A technical strategy connects a real diagnosis to a small set of choices. It gives teams direction while leaving implementation details open to learning.

<Hint>
  A roadmap lists work. A strategy explains which constraints matter, what you
  will optimize, and why the actions reinforce one another.
</Hint>

## Diagnose the situation

Describe the business objective, current system behavior, organizational constraints, and the few obstacles that prevent progress. Separate evidence from assumptions. A diagnosis that names everything creates no focus.

## Set a guiding policy

State how decisions will be made under those constraints. For example: move traffic incrementally, preserve one source of truth, and require measurable rollback at every cutover. The policy should rule out tempting but unsafe options.

## Choose coherent actions

Select actions that reinforce the policy: ownership boundaries, enabling platform work, migration phases, validation signals, and explicit non-goals. Name leading indicators and outcome metrics so the strategy can change when reality disagrees with it.

## Review it as a decision system

Ask what new evidence would change the direction, who owns each decision, and when the strategy will be revisited. Strategy is a maintained model, not a one-time presentation.
